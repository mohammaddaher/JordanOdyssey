Amman

One day in Jordan’s capital

Places to explore

A one-day route: begin at the Citadel in the morning, then walk down to the Roman Theatre and the Nymphaeum in the old downtown.

  • Morning at Amman Citadel

    Start at the Amman Citadel, where the columns of the Temple of Hercules, built in AD 161–166, look out over the city’s hills.

  • Roman Theatre & Odeon

    Visit the Roman Theatre, built in the 2nd century AD in the reign of Antoninus Pius (138–161), and the smaller Odeon beside it, from the same century.

  • Roman Nymphaeum

    See the Roman Nymphaeum in downtown Amman, a large public fountain built in AD 191 at the heart of the old city.

Experiences to try

Souks, knafeh and Arabic coffee

Take time to wander Amman’s lively downtown souks, shop for local crafts and spices, and enjoy a plate of knafeh with a cup of traditional Arabic coffee.

Have more time?

Hejaz Railway Station

Visit the Hejaz Railway Station, an Ottoman stop on the line that reached Amman in 1903. The old station now houses a small railway museum.

Suggested visit
One to two days. The theatre, the Odeon, the Nymphaeum and the souks are close together downtown, and the Citadel is on the hill above.
Getting there
Queen Alia International Airport is about 35 km south of the city. The Hejaz Railway Station is a taxi ride east of downtown.
What to bring
Comfortable shoes for steep streets and stairs. The climb to the Citadel is steep, so a short taxi ride saves your legs.

Entry to the Citadel and the Roman Theatre is included in the Jordan Pass. Opening hours change during the year, so check them before you go.
